# Stations S1–S3 — design, and what each demands of the arm

Phase 1 covers the ribbon-preparation half of the machine: feed, measure, cut,
slit, fan, strip. S4 (crimp) and S5 (insert) are out of scope and gated on
hardware that has not shipped.

**The reason these are designed now, before they are built:** the stations
define what the arm must reach, present, hold and resist. §4 collects those into
an arm specification — that is the real output of this document.

Dimensions live in `sim/layout.py`. Printed parts are generated by
`cad/build_parts.py`.

---

## 1. S1 — feed / measure / cut

The only station with a continuous material path, and the one P0 needs.

### Ribbon path

```
spool → dancer → drive rollers → encoder wheel → PTFE tube → presentation point
 110mm    passive    knurled +      independent    2mm ID       on the bolt
  OD      tension    polyurethane   of the drive                circle
```

### Parts

| Part | Spec | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Spool | 110 mm OD, 31 mm wide, 8 mm bore, printed | Holds 17.9 m against a 15.24 m stock roll. Bore matches Z-stage rod stock |
| Hanger | 150 mm axle height, 3030 mount, printed | Gusseted — a bare 150 mm PLA upright flexes under dancer tension |
| Dancer arm | 70 mm, spring-loaded, printed | Constant light tension regardless of how the roll pays off. Its flag is the spool-empty detect |
| Drive rollers | knurled steel + polyurethane idler, spring preload, NEMA 17 | Pays out **and** pulls back |
| **Measuring wheel** | **31.83 mm dia = 100.00 mm circumference** | See below — this number is chosen, not inherited |
| Encoder | 600 P/R optical, quadrature | Independent of the drive |
| Guide tube | 2 mm ID / 4 mm OD PTFE, printed mount | Sets a repeatable presentation point regardless of spool behaviour |
| Guillotine | hardened blade + anvil, SDA20×20 | Square full-width cut |

### The measuring wheel diameter is a deliberate choice

**31.83 mm diameter gives exactly 100.00 mm of circumference.** With a 600 P/R
encoder in quadrature (2400 counts/rev) that is **0.0417 mm per count**, and
length in millimetres is just `counts × 100 / 2400` — no calibration constant to
get wrong, and two orders of magnitude finer than the ±0.5 mm we need.

Never measure length from drive-roller steps. Ribbon slips against a driven
roller under any tension change, and cable length is the one number the whole
machine is judged on. The encoder wheel rides the ribbon under light spring
preload and does no work, so it does not slip.

### Presentation gap

Tube exit to comb face at the grip position: **8 mm**. Long enough that the comb
never collides with the tube across Z and R tolerance; short enough that ~28 mm
of unsupported ribbon tail cannot buckle when the rollers push it out.

### Cut geometry

The guillotine blade must **shear, not crush**. A crushed ribbon end will not
enter the comb channels — which is the failure that stops the next cycle rather
than the current one, and therefore the expensive kind. Blade rake and a close
anvil clearance matter more than blade force here.

## 2. S2 — split + fan

**The ribbon is designed to be pulled apart by hand.** That is what flat
JR/Futaba servo wire is *for* — you zip the three conductors apart to terminate
them individually. Confirmed from the vendor spec, 2026-07-27.

That single fact reshapes this station. It was scoped as a precision
depth-adjustable two-blade slitting die, with depth control described as "the
whole game": too shallow and it will not zip, too deep and it nicks copper. But
if the web is deliberately weak, we are not cutting through insulation at all —
we are **starting a tear and then letting geometry propagate it.**

### Confirmed ribbon geometry (vendor spec, not measured, not guessed)

| | |
|---|---|
| Conductor OD | **1.40 mm** — listing states "Outer Diameter of Cable: 1.4mm" |
| Conductor pitch | **1.40 mm** — three conductors co-extruded tangent |
| Overall width | **4.20 mm** |
| Strands | 60 × 0.08 mm tinned copper = 0.302 mm² — genuine 22 AWG |
| Insulation | PVC |
| Mass | 250 g / 50 ft = 16.4 g/m |

`cell-design.md` gave the pitch as ~2.5 mm. That was the *connector* cavity
pitch borrowed by mistake, and it made this station look harder than it is —
webs at ±0.7 mm from centreline, not ±1.25 mm.

### Revised approach: splitting wedge, not slitting die

| | Original | Revised |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanism | 2 blades, depth-adjustable to 0.05 mm | Fixed splitting wedge, or a pull-apart pin pair |
| Critical control | Blade depth | Tear start and propagation length |
| Failure if wrong | Nicked copper → crimp fails a pull test two stations later | Split wanders or runs long |
| Actuator | Pneumatic cylinder, precise | Possibly none — the feed stroke can drive the ribbon onto a fixed wedge |
| Cost | ~$32 | Likely printed only |

**The wedge can be a fixed, printed part** that the ribbon is pushed onto by the
feed rollers, with the split length set by how far the arm advances rather than
by tooling geometry. If that works it removes a cylinder, a valve, two flow
controls, and the tightest tolerance in the station.

### What still genuinely needs the physical ribbon

Not the pitch — that is settled. Two things:

1. **Tear-start force and whether the split propagates straight** for 25 mm
   without wandering into a conductor. This is a materials behaviour, not a
   dimension, and no datasheet will answer it.
2. **Web thickness at the tangent**, which sets the wedge's leading-edge radius.
   Bounded by construction — it is thinner than the 1.40 mm conductor and thick
   enough to survive handling — but the exact value wants a caliper.

**Test it the moment the ribbon arrives, by hand, before designing tooling:**
zip 25 mm of it apart and watch what the tear does. Five seconds of work that
decides whether this station is a $32 pneumatic die or a printed wedge.

### Fan geometry

Split length 25 mm; outer conductors move `(8 − 1.40) / 2 = 3.30 mm` laterally
each, or `atan(3.30 / 25) = 7.5°`. Gentle — no set in the insulation, no mangled
breakout. **This answers open question 4 in `cell-design.md` §8.**

| Part | Spec | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Splitting wedge | printed, leading edge on the web line | Replaces the two-blade die pending the hand test |
| Spreader plate | diverging slots, 1.40 → 8 mm over 25 mm, printed | 7.5° splay |
| Backing anvil | only if the wedge route fails | Deferred |

## 3. S3 — strip

| Part | Spec | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| V-blade die | 3 positions at 8 mm comb pitch | One stroke scores all three — equal strip length by construction |
| Depth shim | per recipe | Wrong depth = nicked strands = the crimp fails a pull test two stations later |
| Slug chute | printed | Three insulation slugs drop away, not into the mechanism |

Strip length is **2.75 mm** from the conductor tips. **The pull-off is done by
the arm retracting 4 mm** — there is no dedicated actuator, which is a saving,
but it puts a real requirement on the R axis (§4).

## 4. What the stations demand of the arm

This is the point of designing the stations early.

| Requirement | Value | Set by |
|---|---|---|
| θ stops | 7, over a 270° arc | Station count incl. separate reject |
| θ repeatability | better than ±0.2 mm at R₀ = **±0.057°** | Comb must re-find the same presentation point |
| Z travel | 55 mm | Spread of station engagement heights |
| Z per-station repeatability | ±0.1 mm | Slit and strip depth are set by die geometry, but Z error adds to it |
| R stroke | 80 mm | Retracted clearance to engaged |
| **R fine move** | **exactly 4.0 mm under load** | **S3 pull-off** |
| **R thrust** | **≥ 50 N** | Stripping 3 × 22 AWG at ~5–15 N each |
| S stroke | 20 mm, in 8 mm steps | Selects conductor 1/2/3 at comb pitch |
| W | 180°, two positions, hard stops | End B born opposite to end A |
| Body clamp | holds against 50 N axial | Must not let the ribbon slip during pull-off |
| Comb channels | guide, never grip | One conductor must advance independently |

### The two that constrain the design hardest

**R thrust of ~50 N.** Stripping three 22 AWG conductors simultaneously is the
largest force the arm ever applies. A NEMA 17 on a T8 leadscrew clears this
comfortably — a 2 mm lead gives a large mechanical advantage — but it rules out
a belt-driven R axis, which would need a much larger motor to hit the same
thrust without slipping teeth. **R is a leadscrew, and this is why.**

**The body clamp must not slip.** If the ribbon creeps in the clamp during
pull-off, strip length is wrong *and the measured cable length is wrong*, because
the ribbon has moved relative to the datum the encoder established. A slipping
clamp corrupts the machine's headline spec silently. Serrated pad, and worth
verifying with a pull test during commissioning.

### What the arm-mounted camera is and is not for here

Each station carries an AprilTag, so the camera measures actual pose versus
commanded at every stop — turning droop, detent error and belt backlash from
*assumed repeatable* into *measured and corrected*.

But tag pose at this working distance with a 3.6 mm lens is realistically
**±0.5–1 mm**. That is good for station registration, presence checks, and
verifying an operation happened (did the ribbon get gripped, did the slit cut,
are three tails in three channels). It is **not** a substitute for the
±0.057° mechanical repeatability above. Two different jobs.

## 5. Open

1. **Web thickness** between conductors — sets slit blade depth, and it is the
   single number S2 most depends on. Measure on arrival.
2. **Insulation pull-off force** for this specific ribbon — the 5–15 N per
   conductor above is a class estimate. Measure it, because it sizes the R axis
   and the body clamp together.
3. **Guillotine blade geometry** — rake angle and anvil clearance to shear
   rather than crush. Likely a trial-and-error item on scrap.
4. **Spreader entry geometry** — the funnel that gets a 1.45 mm-pitch tail into
   an 8 mm-pitch channel is the fiddliest printed part in Phase 1.
